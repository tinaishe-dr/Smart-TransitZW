# Smart-TransitZW
Zimbabwe's urban public transport system, largely composed of the formal ZUPCO network and a dominant informal sector of commuter omnibuses ("kombis") and unregistered "mushikashika" operators, suffers from a deep disconnect between the three groups who depend on it: commuters, operators, and the administrations meant to regulate them.
Commuters face unpredictable wait times, inconsistent fares, overcrowding, and safety risks, with no reliable way to know when or whether a vehicle will arrive on a given route. Operators, many working informally with little to no digital infrastructure, lack tools to plan routes efficiently, track vehicle usage, or communicate reliably with passengers or regulators, leaving them exposed to revenue leakage, unsafe competition for passengers ("racing" for fares), and vulnerability to arbitrary enforcement. Administrations and regulators, meanwhile, operate with minimal real-time visibility into route coverage, vehicle numbers, or commuter demand, making policy and infrastructure decisions reactively rather than on evidence, a gap worsened by the fragmented, largely undocumented nature of the informal transport sector.
The result is a system where each stakeholder group optimizes for its own short-term survival, commuters seeking any available ride, operators competing for fares, and regulators enforcing rules with incomplete data, rather than the sector functioning as a coordinated whole. This misalignment contributes to inefficient route coverage, revenue loss, safety incidents, and commuter frustration, while leaving policymakers without the data needed to plan meaningful reform.
This project proposes a digital platform that bridges these three entities, giving commuters real-time visibility into transport availability, giving operators tools to manage routes and vehicles more efficiently, and giving administrations aggregated, anonymized data to inform regulation and infrastructure investment, creating a feedback loop that benefits all parties rather than pitting them against each other.
